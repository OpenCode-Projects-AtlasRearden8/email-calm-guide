Template library

12 fill-in reply scripts, from “yes” to “out of office”. Every yellow blank is yours to fill in. Pick one, copy it, trim it, send it — a 30-second reply beats a perfect one you never send.

The copy buttons work locally on your machine: nothing is sent anywhere. Print this page if it helps; the blanks read like a paper form.

Yes with a date

Use when: You can and want to do it, and the ask needs a concrete commitment.

Tone: Warm, certain, one sentence of logistics. Send in under a minute.

Subject: Re: subject

Happy to! I can have it to you by date or day, and it will include the piece you're promising, in five words.

If anything that could change the timing turns up, I'll flag it here rather than go quiet.

Anything else useful to know before I start?

Not now

Use when: You want to help, but not this week — and you're tired of pretending otherwise.

Tone: Gracious, honest, with a real date. Four sentences, no essay.

Subject: Re: subject

Thanks for thinking of me for this. I can't give it real attention until the week or date you actually mean available, and I'd rather tell you that straight than go quiet.

If that timing works, I'll start on day and you'll have deliverable by date.

If there's any way to hand it to someone who could take it sooner, tell me and I'll say yes to that without sulking.

Either way — thanks for asking me.

A kind no

Use when: The answer is no. Cleanup-free, essay-free, and meant to hold up over time.

Tone: Gratitude, the no, one redirect if you have one. Stop there. No apology essay.

Subject: Re: subject

Thank you for asking me — genuinely, it's a thoughtful ask in two words.

I'm not able to take it on right now. My week / month / plate is spoken for, and this deserves better than leftover attention.

If it helps: optional redirect — another name, a later quarter, or "not for me" — or leave out this line. And if it comes up again at a better time, I'd be glad to hear about it then.

Again, thanks for the ask.

Handing off to the right person

Use when: You're not the fixer here, but you know who is — and you want to stop being the middle of the thread.

Tone: Efficient and warm, 20 seconds of your life. Adds one warm line so nobody feels bounced.

Subject: Re: subject

Good question for name, who owns the area in three words. Copying them here so you don't need a second round trip.

Name, is this something you can help with by date, if there is one? All the context is in the thread below.

Optional warm line: thanks both — this one's important to me / nice to pass good problems to good people.

Quick update

Use when: You owe somebody a status and it's been more days than you'd like anyone to know.

Tone: Two honest lines. Sixty seconds. A short update now beats a perfect report next month.

Subject: Quick update on the thing

Quick update on the thing:

It's on track / slower than planned / waiting on one piece: one sentence a stranger could picture, no jargon.

Next step is the actual next action, which I'll finish by day. Nothing you need to do unless one thing changes.

Happy to give the longer version when it's useful.

Checking in

Use when: Someone owes you an answer, it's gone past the date, and you'd like to not seethe about it.

Tone: Assume good faith; restate the ask; make the no easier than the yes. Never a guilt essay.

Subject: Checking in on the thing

Hope your week is a good one. Friendly bump on the thing from the date of your earlier email — no worries if it's fallen down the stack.

Short version of the ask: what you need, restated in one sentence.

If the timing's off, just say and I'll work around it — a "not now" from you is genuinely helpful too.

Asking for something

Use when: You need a thing from someone — a favor, a document, a decision — and want the yes to be easy.

Tone: Context up front, ask in bold strokes, deadline visible. The shorter the ask, the faster the yes.

Subject: Request: what you need in three words

Quick request that should be easy to either grant or turn down:

What you need, in one short sentence.

Context: one line of why, only as much as it helps. If it's nothing, write "No context needed — you've got the full picture already."

By when: date / "whenever suits this month". And if the answer is no or not-now, a one-line reply is a complete answer and much appreciated.

Thanks!

No reply needed

Use when: You received information and the sender deserves a loop-closed acknowledgement, not a conversation.

Tone: Six seconds. Two lines. This is a receipt, not a chat.

Subject: Re: subject

Got it, thanks — that answers my question / works on my end / is what I needed.

Nothing needed from me right now; I'll shout if anything changes.

— name

Can we meet

Use when: You need actual time with someone, and calendar ping-pong is the thing you're avoiding.

Tone: Propose, don't interrogate. One option first, a graceful fallback second. Deadline visible.

Subject: Meeting: topic in two words (length in minutes min)

Could we grab length minutes about topic? Optional line of agenda, so nobody prepares blindly.

To keep the ping-pong short, I'll propose day, time, your zone — if that works, say "yes" and it's real. If not, two more options: option two, or option three.

If none of those fit, send me one of your slots and I'll make it work. If a call/walk-and-talk suits better than a meeting, equally good on my end.

Sorry for the slow reply

Use when: The reply aged. The email deserves an answer; the apology should not outlive the answer.

Tone: One honest line of apology, then the actual answer. Never two paragraphs of contrition.

Subject: Re: subject

Sorry for the slow reply — one clean line of truth: the week got loud / this fell down my stack / no excuse, plain drift. Your email mattered and it deserved faster.

The answer: the actual reply content, starting with the direct answer, then the one-line of context if needed.

What happens next: next step / "all yours" / a date.

Thanks for your patience.

Declining an invitation

Use when: You can't (or don't want to) attend, and you'd like to keep the relationship warm.

Tone: Grateful, clear, one line of future-warmth if you mean it. No invented excuses ever.

Subject: Re: invitation

Thank you for the invite to the event in three words — that's kind of you to think of me.

I can't make it this time: one honest line — that week's already full / keeping my evenings quiet for a bit / not my thing, but thank you.

Optional line of warmth you actually mean: next time, count me in / hope it's wonderful / coffee that week instead?

Have a great one.

Away till (out of office)

Use when: You'll be slow or offline, and strangers deserve a date instead of silence.

Tone: Four lines. A date, a person for urgent things, no itinerary, no apologizing for having a life.

Subject: Away till date (slower replies)

Hi there! I'm away from email until date with limited (or no) access in the meantime.

For anything urgent about topic, please contact name at contact — they can help faster than I can from where I am.

I'll reply when I'm back, even if that takes a couple of days. Thanks for your patience!

Name
