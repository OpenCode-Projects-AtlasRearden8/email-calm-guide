Digging out (firehose edition)

For the days you open the inbox and the unread counter has thousands, or the folder has four years of sediment. No shame — this happens to surgeons, CEOs, and everyone's aunt. Here's the rescue plan, in the order that actually works.

Step 0: the holding reply (5 minutes, do it first)

If anyone is waiting on you and you've gone quiet, send the cheapest possible reset — chapter 5's ten-second version of "sorry for the slow reply" does it:

Hi name — I'm digging out from a big email backlog. I'll give your message the attention it deserves by day/week. If it's urgent before then, tell me again here and I'll bump it to the front.

Send to everyone waiting within the last month (search is:unread older_than:30d, or just the senders you remember). That single paragraph converts guilt promises into a real deadline, buys you permission to do the work in the open, and prevents "did you fall off the map?" follow-ups while you dig.

Step 1: renounce the past (the big archive)

Old email isn't a library; it's sediment. Don't read it, don't organize it — decide what date the world restarted and archive everything before it:

  • Pick a restart line: "everything before the 1st gets archived" or "everything older than 60 days".
  • Select all mail older than that → Archive (search before:2024/01/01 style queries in Gmail, or sort by date and select-a-block elsewhere). Don't open any of it — if something in there were urgent, they'd have sent a new email by now or they will phone.
  • Everything before your restart line gets the search test later if needed: it's still findable by sender, subject, or quoted words. Deleted? Never. Just removed from tomorrow's emotional foreground.

This step alone clears most of the stack in about two guilt-free minutes. What remains is genuinely current.

Step 2: run one normal tidy

The next-value inbox is small again. Do the ten-minute tidy exactly as described in chapter 3 (newest screen first), with the timer. You're operating the calm system inside a calm system now; no special rescue rules apply.

Step 3: start the tidy habit before the next flood

The rescue only sticks if the daily inflow is down to a ten-minute tidy. Floods have causes, and yours is usually one of the three from earlier chapters: no newsletter rule doing the filing (ch. 8), no daily window at an actual time of day you're free (move it next to an anchor like first coffee, after lunch, before closing the laptop), or one sender that's about to strike a third time (ch. 4).

What rescue is not

  • Not a deep-clean. Don't open, don't archive-with-categories, don't read "just this one from mom from 2021". The past is past; the future happens this week.
  • Not a one-time monument. A rescue that isn't followed by the tidy habit becomes a twice-yearly tradition, which is much worse than the habit.
  • Not about being caught up by Friday. Being operational by tomorrow matters; being empty doesn't exist in real life.

If you're truly flooded right now and reading this at 11pm

Do Steps 0 and 1 only. Holding replies + the big archive take fifteen minutes total, and they stop the bleed — which is enough for tonight. The rest is genuinely better tomorrow with a timer.

If you do only one thing: the holding reply in step 0. It's the smallest message that matches reality, and there is no calmer sentence in email than "I'll get you a real answer by Friday."
