Start here

Welcome. This is a manual for people who feel that little jolt of dread when the mail chimes. Not a productivity cult, not a system with a decade-long certification track. Just a friend walking you through a calmer way to deal with email, one ten-minute window at a time.

Here's the promise up front: you don't have to be fast, disciplined, or organized to get email under control. You need a short daily routine you can repeat half-asleep, three folders instead of forty, and a few replies you can send before you've had time to overthink them. That's this book.

The whole idea in one breath

Email feels endless because it is endless. The goal isn't an empty inbox forever — nobody's stays empty. The goal is a small, predictable amount of attention, spent on purpose, at a time you choose:

  1. Catch it fast. Ten minutes a day on the inbox, usually morning, always with a timer. That's "the ten-minute tidy" in chapter 3.
  2. Keep three folders. Needs action, waiting on someone, someday. Everything else gets archived and found easily later. That's chapter 2.
  3. Reply short and kind. Most emails deserve three sentences, and "short" is not rude. Chapters 5 and 6 have ready-to-copy templates.
  4. Let the robots do the boring parts. Filing rules, auto-replies, and — if you want it — a local AI helper that drafts and shortens on your own machine (chapter 7), which pairs with kind-email-crafter-cli.
  5. Guard the door. Dread makes people click things they shouldn't. Chapter 9 is a short list of scam-resistant habits that work even on a bad day.

How to use this manual

Start with chapters 2 and 3 — that's the spine: folders plus a daily ten-minute tidy. Everything else you can read in whatever order the mess is coming from. Short on time? Read 2, 3, and 10 (the weekly reset) and call it a day.

Each chapter ends with the same little box: If you only do one thing, do that. The whole manual is designed so missing a day (or a week) doesn't break anything.

If you're drowning right now and clicking around for help as we speak, skip to chapter 12, "Digging out".

Who this is for

  • Normally-tired people, tired people, and extremely tired people (hello, night-shifters: email is not a pager, and nothing in here assumes you reply within the hour).
  • Small-business owners and side-hustlers who need receipts, orders, and invoices findable, and want to answer strangers kindly and quickly.
  • Anyone whose inbox has become a tiny cold slideshow of their own failures.

One honest note from the usual boilerplate: this is a practical guide, not professional, medical, or legal advice. It also won't negotiate with your spam folder on your behalf — you remain the grown-up in the room.

A quick word on privacy

This guide (and its companion tools) is local-first. Everything happens on your machine: no accounts, no API keys, no analytics, no "sync with somebody's cloud". The optional AI helper in chapter 7 runs locally too, using a small model on your own computer, so your emails stay yours.

If you only do one thing: create the three folders in chapter 2. Two minutes, no reading required, and every other chapter gets easier.
