# The weekly reset

Ten-to-fifteen minutes, once a week. Where the ten-minute tidy keeps each day under control, the weekly reset is what keeps *the system itself* honest — folders tidy, nudges sent, one rule improved. Put it in the calendar as a real meeting with yourself; that's the only scheduling trick that works.

## The checklist

Work through it briskly, that's the point. Each box is one small verdict, not a project.

- [ ] **Needs action.** Anything older than a week? Send one of: a dated promise, a kind no, or archive it with a clear conscience — the only bad answer is silently keeping it.
- [ ] **Waiting on.** Pick up to three people/businesses you actually need an answer from — send the gentle nudge (template 60). Don't nag the whole folder; the top three get results.
- [ ] **Someday.** Ten-second skim. Anything gone properly dusty (three weekly resets without a glance)? Delete or unsubscribe — the same three-strikes rule as chapter 4.
- [ ] **Unsubscribe count.** One sender per week, minimum. The reset isn't a ritual; it's weekly turnover.
- [ ] **One rule.** Review a single rule — did the filing behave this week? Adjust the condition, or delete if it's dead weight. You'll have all your rules audited in a month without ever doing a marathon.
- [ ] **Calendar peek.** Anything arriving next week that generates email (a deadline, a trip, an exam week)? Ten seconds of looking now prevents Friday's surprise.
- [ ] **Templates tidy.** Did you write a reply this week that worked well? Save its shape into the template library (or a note) — your reply bank grows.
- [ ] **Next week's tone.** One sentence to yourself: what's the one email you're *not* looking forward to? Schedule its reply — don't let it ambush Monday-you.

That's the whole reset. Fifteen minutes on a Sunday evening (or first Monday coffee) and the whole start of next week is *already lighter*.

## If skipping a week happens

Then you skip a week. The system doesn't punish; the folders just hold things steady until you're back. Two weeks in a row? Run the reset for real. Three? That's not a moral failing, that's a busy season — and the next tidy gives you the same amount of reset credit anyway. The one thing *never* to do is wait for "a good week" to get back to the habit; this whole system exists precisely because good weeks aren't dependable.

## Why "reset" and not "zero"

Naming is a small kindness to yourself. A *reset* is something that happens again and again — a fresh start that carries no score. What the reset protects is not an empty inbox but a *predictable* one: you always know where things stand and where they're heading.

> **If you only do one thing:** the first two boxes — Needs action older than a week, and up to three *checking in* nudges. Those two boxes are where dropped-thing regret actually comes from.
