# Reply templates

These are the fill-in-the-blank versions of chapter 5. You don't read this chapter so much as raid it: copy a template, fill the blanks, adjust one line if the situation calls for it, send.

The full set lives in two places — they're the same files:

- **On the page:** the built site has a *Template library* page (see `site/templates.html` after building) with copy buttons for each.
- **In the repo:** `templates/replies/*.md` — plain files, no lock-in, so any tool (including kind-email-crafter-cli) can read them.

## How the templates work

The **yellow blanks** marked like `__this__` are the only things you add: a date, a name, a one-line status. The rest of the wording is deliberately spare and warm, so a light edit keeps it sounding like you. Two small rules:

- Fill, glance, send — a ten-second read catches the occasional blank your fingers skipped.
- Templates are drafts, not autopilot. If a reply needs a human sentence, add it — one honest line beats four polished ones.

## The five you'll use most

When you're staring at a message and the dread hum starts, these cover most days:

### The 20, "Not now"

Someone wants something; you can't do it *today*; you don't want to pretend it's gone. Give it a real future date you actually believe, not a heroic one.

### The 30, "A kind no"

Boundaries in one paragraph: gratitude, the no, any light redirect, no apology essay. People respect a clean no far more than a slow maybe.

### The 50, "Quick update"

You owe someone a status and it's been more days than you'd like. One line on how it's going, one on the next step. Sixty seconds, loop closed, reputation intact.

### The 60, "Checking in"

Someone owes *you* something. The nudge that doesn't seethe: assume good faith, restate the ask, offer to drop it if it's not the right time. It works on vendors, colleagues, and cousins.

### The 90, "Sorry for the slow reply"

For the reply that aged. One line of honesty, then straight to the answer. Don't make the apology longer than the email itself.

## Why templates, of all things

Because at the tired end of a shift, or the bottom of a bad week, *deciding* is the expensive part. Templates pre-decide the shape so you supply only the facts. They also quietly protect you from writing the over-long apology or the too-fancy whatever — the templates are already kid-gloves calibrated.

## Pairs with kind-email-crafter-cli

This manual's companion tool, kind-email-crafter-cli, reads the same `templates/replies/` files and drafts replies locally — same blanks, same tone, no cloud. If you have it installed, run it and pick a template; the AI drafts into the blanks and you edit before sending. If you don't have it, everything here still works by hand with copy and paste.

> **If you only do one thing:** go to the template library and copy the *checking in* template for the person you've been avoiding. You know the one.
