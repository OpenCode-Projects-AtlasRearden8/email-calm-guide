# The ten-minute tidy

This is the daily habit the whole manual hangs on. One timer, ten minutes, four moves. It works because it's small enough to actually happen — including on the days you've got nothing left.

## The setup

- A timer on your phone or computer, set to ten minutes. Annoying little ticking ones work best, because "vibing in the inbox for an hour" is the enemy.
- Your three folders from chapter 2 ready to receive things.
- Notifications off for the other 23 hours and 50 minutes of the day (chapter 8 shows how to automate the common cases; for now, turning them off is the move).

Do it at roughly the same time each day — first coffee works for most people, lunch works for shift workers, "after the school run" works for parents. Same time beats perfect timing.

## The four moves

Go through today's mail (top of the list down, or just the unread) and give every message one of four verdicts. No message gets two verdicts. No verdict is "park it till next week."

1. **Reply now** — if the honest answer takes under two minutes, write it and send it. Three sentences maximum; chapter 5 explains why that's plenty, chapter 6 gives you the words.
2. **Move it** — if it needs real work later, it goes to **Needs action**. If you're waiting on someone else, **Waiting on**. If it's a "someday read", **Someday**.
3. **Archive it** — receipts, chains you were only CC'd on, threads that finished without you. Gone from the landing strip, still searchable forever. This is the move that makes the number go down; use it generously.
4. **Unsubscribe** — actual junk mail that snuck in: one click, then archive. Chapter 4 does this guilt-free.

When the timer ends, you stop. Mid-message? It goes to Needs action and tomorrow's-you handles it. The timer is what makes this sustainable; honor it even when it feels rude.

## If your inbox is a landslide

Ten minutes can't empty four thousand messages, and that's fine — that's a different job, and chapter 12 is the excavation plan. For today:

- Triage only the **newest screen** (the top of the list). Leave the older slide untouched; it gets handled in the rescue, not in the daily tidy.
- Or triage only **unread**, if unread is a meaningful number for you.

The tidy's job is *stopping the bleeding*, not reconstructing the ankle. Slow the inflow, get today under control, then excavate on your schedule.

## What "done" looks like

Not zero. Done looks like this: the inbox holds nothing you're pretending not to see; Needs action is a list you can face; Waiting on is a list you can nudge; and you have a rough idea what tomorrow needs. Some days the inbox still shows 15 unread at the end. That's a win, not a failure, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling a course.

> **If you only do one thing:** set a 10-minute timer tomorrow morning and use it only for *replies* — every other verdict can wait a day. One habit, politely installed, beats four done once.
