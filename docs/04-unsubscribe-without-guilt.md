# Unsubscribe without guilt

Newsletters are the paper cuts of the inbox: individually harmless, collectively why email hurts. This chapter gives you permission (you don't need it, but it helps) and a method.

## The permission slip

Here it is, in writing:

> You are allowed to leave any email list, for any reason, at any time, without telling anyone goodbye. Even lists you *chose* once. Even from charities you support. Even from your cousin's sourdough blog.

Unsubscribing costs one click. Staying subscribed costs several minutes a day, forever, plus the quiet dread noise. The math isn't close.

And to defuse the guilt spiral: unsubscribing is not a statement about the sender. It's a statement about your week. Companies send to *everyone until told otherwise*; the click is just you answering a question they asked.

## The three-strikes rule

You don't have to make a big decision each time. Use this simple audit when you triage:

**If you've skipped the last 3 emails from a sender, you're not their audience anymore — unsubscribe.**

Scan the subject lines in your inbox and any "someday" pile. Three silent skip-streaks per sender is the tripwire. Some judgment calls:

- **Bank statements, receipts, order confirmations, appointment reminders:** keep. These are transactional, and you won't "get around to" re-finding them later.
- **Newsletters, marketing, "checking in to see if you saw...", webinar promos:** three strikes, gone.
- **Group threads** (school, union, neighborhood): not newsletters; move to Someday or archive; don't unsubscribe if the group needs you reachable.

## How to unsubscribe (30 seconds each)

1. **Use the real link.** Scroll to the bottom of the email, tap *Unsubscribe*, and follow the one click through. Gmail and Outlook also show an "Unsubscribe" button near the sender's name for senders that support it — same mechanism, same safety.
2. **Never fill in a reason.** Leave it blank; close the tab. Unsubscribing is not a breakup; you owe nobody a paragraph.
3. **One per day is plenty.** During the tidy, unsubscribe from a single sender and archive the rest. Even a single sitting clears dozens in a week.
4. **Delete the backlog afterwards.** The confirmation email and the old backlog can all be archived at once; select, archive, exhale. Unsubscribing stops the inflow; archiving removes the weight.

## The one big caveat: sketchy senders

If the email looks phony instead of merely annoying (spoofed brand, "you have won", your bank "verifying"), don't tap the link in it at all — unsubscribe links in scam mail are a known trap. Treat it as spam: report as spam/junk instead of clicking around inside it. Chapter 9 has the red-flag checks.

## If you might truly want some of it

One honest exception: a handful of newsletters you *do* want but don't want interrupting you. Set a rule (chapter 8) that skips the inbox and files them straight to **Someday**. You'll read them on your terms — and if "your terms" turns out to be never, the three-strikes rule still applies, same permission slip as everyone else.

> **If you only do one thing:** pick the single most-ignored newsletter in your inbox and unsubscribe from it right now. That's the whole habit: one click at a time, and no goodbye notes.
