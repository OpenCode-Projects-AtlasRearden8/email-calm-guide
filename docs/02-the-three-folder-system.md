# The three-folder system

Most email folders fail from being honest at the start — "I'll organize everything someday!" — and they work by defining the inbox as a landing strip, not a home, with three folders catching the short-term stuff.

**Rule: the inbox is a landing strip, not a home.** Nothing lives there. Three folders catch the short-term stuff.

## The three folders

### 1. Needs action

The work queue. Messages that require something from you: a reply, a decision, a form, a signature. Any name that tells the truth works:

`Needs action` / `Reply to-do` / `Do this week`

This folder should stay small — a small handful of items, *usually under ten messages*. If it keeps growing past one screen, that's a signal to trim, defer, or say no to some of it, not an excuse to make a fourth folder.

### 2. Waiting on

For the email you sent where you're now waiting on someone else — a quote, a document, a permission. You asked; they haven't answered yet. Keep those here.

This folder is the one that ends the classic embarrassment: was it they who ghosted, me who forgot, or did I already archive it? The email sits in one obvious place until the deal either closes or the "nudge later" moment arrives (a polite nudge is the *checking in* template in chapter 6).

### 3. Someday

This is the *honest* junk drawer for things you may genuinely want later:

- newsletters you actually want to read someday
- a webinar recording you didn't watch
- the "what to do if your card is stolen" forwarded thing

Keep it here if you like; once a week the reset (chapter 10) audits this folder for anything that has gone properly dusty.

## Why only three folders?

Because you can't answer the question "where should this go?" quickly with more than three options. Decades of inbox systems have died on the filing step. Three folders keeps it small; if something arguably belongs in two of them, put it in Needs action — you're the boss.

## Setting them up in your mail client

- **Gmail:** Settings (gear) → See all settings → Labels → Create new label. Same labels work as folders for the tidy: drag an email onto a label in the sidebar.
- **Outlook:** right-click the folder list → New folder. Same thing.
- **Apple Mail:** Mailbox → New Mailbox (or File → New Mailbox in older versions).

You don't need color, priorities, or nested hierarchies. Make three folders; two minutes.

## How the tidy works with the folders

Every email gets one decision, in chapter 3's ten minutes:

- **Less than two minutes of work?** Do it now, archive it today.
- **Needs action later?** Into Needs action.
- **Waiting on a person?** Into Waiting on.
- **Not happening** (newsletters, FYIs, completed threads): archive.
- **Might read someday?** Into Someday.

Archive everything — with search nothing is lost, because your provider will find it by sender, subject, or a quoted word. That's the whole trick: **you only really own three folders.** The rest of your mail is a searchable database.

> **If you only do one thing:** create the folders. Rename as you please — the names matter less than having places for "act now" and "waiting on" and "someday". These few minutes are the whole project.
